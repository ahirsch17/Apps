## Slide 6: The Solution

**Between shows you who's available, when it matters.**

Not everyone. Not always.

**Just the people in your orbit, at the moment you could actually meet up.**

### Core Features:
1. **Who's free right now?** (real-time availability)
2. **Who's in my classes?** (context for reaching out)
3. **What's happening tonight?** (events + who's going)
4. **Where are my friends?** (passive location, not tracking)

## Slide 9: Why It Works

### The Psychology:

**Removes barriers**:
- ❌ "What if they're busy?" → ✅ You already know they're free
- ❌ "What if they say no?" → ✅ Low commitment ("I'm going anyway")
- ❌ "Do I have a reason to text?" → ✅ Context ("I'm at Starbucks too")
- ❌ "Should I plan something?" → ✅ Spontaneous, no planning

**Creates serendipity**:
- "I didn't know you were on campus!"
- "I didn't know you were in my class!"
- "I didn't know you were free at the same time!"

**Authentic behavior**:
- GenZ doesn't plan ("let's hang Tuesday at 3pm")
- GenZ vibes ("wya" at 11pm)
- Between enables the behavior they already want

| Solution | What It Does | Why It's Not Enough |
|----------|-------------|-------------------|
| **GroupMe/iMessage** | Group chat | "Anyone free?" → silence or too late |
| **Snap Maps** | Location sharing | Shows WHERE, not WHEN/WHY |

| **Instagram** | Social posts | Async, performative, not real-time |
| **Clubs** | Organized activities | Once a week, 50 people, forced |
| **Tinder/Bumble BFF** | Stranger matching | High friction, awkward, time-consuming |

### Our Advantage:
- **Context**: You're in the same class, dorm, major
- **Low commitment**: See availability without asking
- **Real-time**: Not planning, not async, RIGHT NOW
- **Actionable**: Built-in reason to reach out

**We're not competing with social apps. We're solving a logistics problem.**

## Slide 19: Risk Mitigation

### Key Risks & Our Approach:

**1. Privacy Concerns**
- Risk: Students uncomfortable sharing location
- Mitigation: Opt-in, building-level only, ghost mode, clear controls

**2. Chicken-and-egg (Network Effects)**
- Risk: Not useful until critical mass
- Mitigation: Start with dense populations (dorms, large classes)

**3. University Approval**
- Risk: Schools block or don't integrate
- Mitigation: Start student-led, prove value, then pitch to admin

**4. Competition (Big Tech Copies)**
- Risk: Snap or Instagram builds this
- Mitigation: University partnerships = moat, domain expertise

**5. Behavior Change Required**
- Risk: Students don't adopt new habits
- Mitigation: Meets existing behavior (already checking Snap Maps)
