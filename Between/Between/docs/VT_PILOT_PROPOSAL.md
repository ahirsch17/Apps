## Executive Summary

**The Problem**: Virginia Tech loses 300 freshmen each year (~5%), representing $48M in lifetime tuition. National studies show 50% of college students experience loneliness, a leading predictor of dropout.

**The Root Cause**: Students struggle to form connections not because they don't want friends, but because they can't find the people who'd want to hang out with them—even when those people are 5 minutes away.

**The Solution**: Between is campus-specific social infrastructure that makes students visible to each other when it matters, enabling spontaneous connection.

**The Proposal**: FREE pilot in Pritchard Hall (500 freshmen) for Fall 2027 semester. Zero cost, measurable outcomes, no obligation.

**Expected Impact**: 30% decrease in loneliness scores, measurable improvement in freshman experience, and success stories that strengthen VT's retention and admissions efforts.

**If successful**: Expand to full freshman class with paid contract ($150K/year) starting Spring 2028.

## Part 1: The Problem Virginia Tech Faces

### The Retention Challenge

**Current State**:
- VT freshman retention: ~95% (above national avg of 70%)
- Still lose ~300 students per year
- Cost per dropout: $160K (4 years × $40K tuition)
- **Annual impact: $48M in lost lifetime tuition**

**Why Students Leave**:
According to research (Tinto, 1993; Astin, 1984):
1. Academic struggles (20%)
2. Financial issues (30%)
3. **Social isolation (50%)** ← The biggest factor

**The Social Isolation Problem**:
- Students who make friends in first 6 weeks: 95% retention
- Students who DON'T make friends: 60% retention
- **Difference: 35 percentage points**

### The Mental Health Crisis

**National Data** (ACHA, 2023):
- 50% of students feel lonely
- 60% experience anxiety
- 40% report depression
- Counseling wait times: 4-8 weeks

**VT's Response**:
- Cook Counseling Center overwhelmed
- Hiring more counselors ($80K+ each)
- Wellness programs and campaigns
- Total spend: $5M+ annually

**The Gap**: You're treating symptoms (depression, anxiety) but not the root cause (loneliness, isolation).

### Current Solutions Fall Short

**Orientation Week**:
- ✅ Good: Gets students excited
- ❌ Problem: Only lasts 1 week, then what?

**RA Programs**:
- ✅ Good: RAs are trained to help
- ❌ Problem: 1 RA can't be everywhere for 50 students

**Clubs & Organizations**:
- ✅ Good: Shared interests
- ❌ Problem: 50+ people, once a week, forced timing

**Roommate Pairing**:
- ✅ Good: Built-in connection
- ❌ Problem: Luck of the draw, 50% compatibility rate

**The Insight**: These solutions work for SOME students, but 30% fall through the cracks. Those 30% are your 300 dropouts.

## Part 2: The Solution - Between

### What It Is

Between is **social infrastructure** for college campuses—like WiFi or gym access, but for human connection.

**Core Function**: Makes students visible to each other at the moment it matters.

**How It Works**:

**Emma's Story** (Typical Pritchard Freshman):
```
Tuesday, 11:45am
Emma just got out of CHEM 1035
She's hungry but doesn't want to eat alone (again)

Opens Between:
┌─────────────────────────────────┐
│ Free right now (3)              │
│ ├── Sarah - D2                  │
│ ├── Mike - Squires              │
│ └── Rachel - Owens              │
│                                 │
│ Next class: CALC - 1:00pm       │
└─────────────────────────────────┘

Emma texts Sarah: "yo you at d2?"
Sarah: "yeah come thru!"

First lunch with someone in 2 weeks.
```

**By Week 3**: Emma, Sarah, and Mike have a recurring M/W/F lunch crew.  
**By Week 4**: Emma isn't lonely anymore.  
**By Week 8**: Emma doesn't even use the app (she has her people).

**That's success.**

### What Makes It Different

| Traditional Solutions | Between |
|-----------------------|---------|
| Scheduled events | Real-time, spontaneous |
| Large groups (50+) | Small, organic groups (2-4) |
| Weekly commitments | Low/no commitment |
| Forced interaction | Natural discovery |
| Limited reach (club members) | All students |
| Hard to measure | Data-driven |

**Key Insight**: Students don't plan ("Let's hang Tuesday at 3pm"). They vibe ("wya right now?"). Between enables the behavior they already want.

### Privacy & Safety

**Campus-Only**:
- VT students only (verified via vt.edu email)
- No strangers, no randos
- Mutual friend network

**Privacy Controls**:
- Opt-in location (building-level, not GPS)
- Ghost mode (hide from everyone)
- Block/report features
- No public profiles

**Data Security**:
- Encrypted schedules (we never see raw class data)
- FERPA compliant
- No data selling
- Admin dashboard (aggregated only)

### Why Students Will Actually Use It

**Unlike wellness apps** (10% adoption, drop off after 2 weeks):

**✅ Solves a real pain point**: "I'm hungry but don't want to eat alone"  
**✅ No stigma**: It's a social app, not therapy  
**✅ Immediate value**: Results in first week  
**✅ Viral**: Friends invite friends  
**✅ Free**: No payment friction  

**Expected adoption**: 40-60% in pilot dorm (based on similar apps at other schools)

## Part 7: Frequently Asked Questions

### "Why should we trust a startup?"

Fair question. We're not asking you to trust us with your entire freshman class on day one. We're proposing a small, controlled pilot in one dorm. If it doesn't work, you stop. If it does, you expand.

**Risk mitigation**: Free pilot = zero financial risk. Small scale = limited operational risk.

### "What if students don't use it?"

That's the point of the pilot—to find out. But based on similar apps at other schools (e.g., Jungle at Columbia, Pear at Stanford), we expect 40-60% adoption in a tight-knit dorm like Pritchard.

If adoption is low, we'll work with you to figure out why (wrong messaging? wrong features?) or conclude it's not the right fit.

### "What about privacy concerns?"

We're FERPA compliant, use encrypted data, and give students full control over what they share. Location is opt-in and building-level only (not GPS tracking).

We can walk through our full privacy architecture in the initial meeting.

### "How is this different from existing solutions?"

**Counseling services**: We're prevention, not treatment. We reduce the number of students who need counseling.

**Orientation programs**: We work year-round, not just week one.

**Clubs**: We enable spontaneous, small-group connection (2-4 people), not scheduled large-group events.

**RAs**: We scale beyond what one RA can do for 50 students.

We complement these solutions—we don't replace them.

### "What's the catch?"

There isn't one. We need proof of concept data to raise funding and approach other schools. You need a solution for freshman loneliness. Our interests are aligned.

If the pilot fails, you learned something and paid nothing.  
If it succeeds, you found a scalable solution to a $48M problem.

### "What if we want to build this ourselves?"

You could, but:
- Cost: $500K+ in development (2-3 engineers for 6-12 months)
- Risk: No guarantee students will use it
- Time: 12-18 months before you see results
- Opportunity cost: Your IT team has other priorities

We've already built it. We'll give you the pilot for free. You'll have results in 6 months, not 18.
